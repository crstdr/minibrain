# Reflect

Use the local operating instructions and memory bindings.

Review relevant recent work against the user's goals, principles and observed outcomes. Look for recurring mistakes, contradictions, missed commitments and context friction. Distinguish evidence from hypotheses.

Choose the smallest useful improvement—or none. Confirm inferred principles before adopting them; propose changes outside your existing authority.

## Where changes belong

- Standing rules → `<principles>` in its canonical instruction source.
- Procedures → the owning operating-instruction section or skill.
- Durable knowledge, decisions and lessons → their existing canonical memory space, preserving scope and evidence.
- Commitments and next actions → their authoritative source, with a pointer in the working index.

Update only what needs changing. These destinations are not a checklist requiring edits to every one.

Persist durable outcomes through the installed checkpoint procedure. Do not create a separate reflection diary or duplicate records. If nothing durable changed, no checkpoint is needed.
